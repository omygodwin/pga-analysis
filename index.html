The Short Version

Hurstbourne is a par-70 test with teeth — only two par 5s, a stack of long par 4s, and small, quick bentgrass greens (~4,750 sq ft) that demand precise irons and a sharp short game. It played firm and brutal in 2025 (~10 under won). This year it's different: rain has already fallen and more is coming, so the course is softer and more “doable,” in the players' own words — receptive greens should pull the winning number lower than last year. Wind is a non-factor; the variable is storm timing, with the heaviest cell reserved for Sunday. With the top of the world at the Genesis Scottish Open, it's a wide-open opposite-field week headlined by two must-watch groups: Max Homa · defending champ William Mouw · Lucas Glover (all three contended at the John Deere last week), and the young-guns trio of Jackson Koivun · Miles Russell · Ben James.

The Draw — where the edge actually is this week

Wind is not the differentiator. With sustained speeds in the 5–10 mph range all four days, no half of the draw gets a meaningful wind break. The variable that matters is storm timing, and that hands the edge to the morning wave.

The concrete read (Thursday): the National Weather Service has showers/thunderstorms "mainly after 2 p.m." with the morning partly sunny and calm. Play starts around 7 a.m. ET (gates 6:45) off split tees in two waves. So Round 1's morning starters get dry, soft, receptive greens before the cells fire; the afternoon wave carries the lightning-delay risk. Waves flip Friday, so the picture reverses.

But it's close to a wash over 36 holes. The waves flip — Thursday's morning starters play Friday afternoon, and vice versa — so each half gets one clean morning and one storm-risk afternoon. The real edge is narrow: Round 1's morning wave gets the first crack at a soft, calm course and dodges any Thursday-afternoon wash-out. See the wave split below.

Sunday is the wild card. The heaviest rain (~1 inch) is forecast for the final round — soft, gettable, target-golf conditions (possible lift-clean-place) for anyone ahead of the worst of it, but real delay risk that can compress the finish. A rain-softened Hurstbourne lets bombers fire at the small greens more freely, nudging the ceiling on low rounds up.

Net read: favor the AM side Thursday, lean into rain-softened scoring, and don't over-price a wind advantage that isn't there.

What Hurstbourne Rewards

the fit that travels here

Iron play & proximity

the separator

Small bentgrass greens make Strokes Gained: Approach the headline skill. Precision and distance control into ~4,750-sq-ft targets is worth more than length. The field's approach leaders — Ben Kohles, Christiaan Bezuidenhout, Austin Eckroat, Nick Dunlap — profile well.

Putting the small, quick greens

the multiplier

Quick, tricky bentgrass rewards proven putters. This is where an elite putter like Denny McCarthy and a red-hot flat-stick (Homa's has sharpened) can turn a good ball-striking week into a win.

Length — helpful, softened

edge, not a cheat code

Length shortens the long par 4s to wedge distance, and the rain-softened setup lets bombers fire at the small greens more freely than last year's firm, running conditions allowed. Still only worth it if you keep it in play.

  • Bogey avoidance on the par 4s. With only two par 5s, the dozen-plus par 4s decide the tournament. Grinding pars on the long ones and picking off the short ones is the winning shape.
  • Short game as the safety net. Miss the small greens — and everyone will — and scrambling around firm surfaces becomes a scoring skill in its own right.
  • Course history, lightly. Repeat-venue names near the top of comp-course models: Taylor Pendrith, Denny McCarthy, Christiaan Bezuidenhout, Steven Fisk, Beau Hossler, Patrick Rodgers, Lucas Glover, Stephan Jaeger.
