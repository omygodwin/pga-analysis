The Course — TPC Twin Cities

An Arnold Palmer scoring course on a former Blaine sod farm: gettable greens, three reachable par 5s and a board that runs deep red — where wedges and water, not the rough, decide it.

Designed by Arnold Palmer and opened in 2000 on a flat former sod farm in Blaine, Minnesota, TPC Twin Cities spent 26 years hosting the Champions Tour before the 3M Open brought the PGA TOUR to town in 2019. For that debut, architect Steve Wenzloff stretched it past 7,400 yards, narrowed the corridors, added bunkering and converted the old par-5 3rd into a 500-yard par 4 (course par 71). What the changes couldn't change is the DNA: water — Rice Creek and a chain of ponds — is in play on the majority of holes, the bentgrass greens are big and receptive, and three reachable par 5s keep the scoreboard red. It is a wedge-and-water course: get the ball in play, wedge it close, and go low. Kurt Kitayama, the defending champion, put it plainly: “the course just kind of fits me.”

A birdie-fest by design
Field birdie rate: 22% · the board runs red

Arnold Palmer routed TPC Twin Cities across a former Blaine sod farm, and the modern setup is a pure scoring course: big bentgrass greens, gettable wedge distances, and three reachable par 5s that hand out a combined ~−1.1 to par. The 12th (51% birdie, 3% eagle), the 6th and the closing 18th are a bonanza — everyone goes low, and the winner has posted 20-plus under in most editions.

The separator is your irons
Approach = the #1 validated skill here (r +0.47)

When the field finds the short grass — and they do, top-9-of-38 findable — the whole test is the wedge and mid-iron into those big greens. Approach play is the single best predictor of scoring at courses like this; length is nearly worthless (distance benefit ranks #34 of 37). Get it close, make the putt, repeat — which is why pure iron players like Corey Conners and Hideki Matsuyama sit near the top of our Venue Fit board.

Water is the danger, not the rough
Fairway-miss penalty ranks #31 of 35 — it barely bites

Rice Creek and a chain of ponds thread the property; the trouble that wrecks a card here is wet, not thick. Historically the rough is forgiving — one of the lowest fairway-miss penalties on Tour. But the 2026 setup has grown teeth. Scottie Scheffler: “Rough is pretty healthy, so fairways are going to be at a premium this week … the biggest challenge would be just getting the ball in play off the tee.”

Where the weather shows up

Toll is the composite playability score over each round's actual play window (wind + gusts + temperature deviation + precipitation) — lower is easier. Same measure as the playability heat maps elsewhere in this doc.

Across these 6 Thursdays, Toll tracks the field average at r = 0.71 and the leader at r = 0.75 — conditions move the top of the board about as much as they move the field. A tougher Thursday here has meant a shallower lead, not just a worse average.

2022 is the outlier that proves it. It played the most testing Thursday on record (Toll 17.2, 10.2 mph) and is the only year the field averaged over par (+1.78). The lead came back to -6, the shallowest of the six, and the top-5 line to just -4 — two shots easier to reach than a normal year.

But calm doesn't guarantee a runaway. 2020 and 2025 played to almost identical Toll (10.3 vs 10.5) and produced very different days: -8 off a -0.57 field average, against -11 off -2.10. Wind is the lever you can see on a forecast; softness, setup and field strength move the number too, and they don't show up in a Toll reading.

Sample caveat. Six editions, and Toll has only ranged 10.3–17.2 — entirely within the benign-to-testing band, never a genuinely brutal day. Both correlations rest on 6 points and 2022 alone is doing much of the work; one windy edition could move them materially. Treat this as descriptive of what has happened here, not as a model.

What TPC Twin Cities Rewards — Tee to Green

A gettable birdie-fest that still separates on ball-striking: findable fairways, receptive greens and three scoring par 5s — but off-the-tee and approach pull the field apart more than a typical stop, while putting and short game matter less.

TPC Twin Cities is the opposite of a brute. Players find the short grass and hit greens at top-9-of-38 rates, the par 5s bleed red numbers, and every par type plays at or below the Tour norm. So separation doesn't come from surviving difficulty — it comes from scoring. And over six years the skills that actually pull this field apart are ball-striking: off the tee (+1.0σ) and approach (+0.4σ) both separate more here than at a typical stop, while putting and around green separate less. Note the distinction — raw length is still nearly worthless (distance benefit ranks near the bottom of the Tour) and the fairway-miss penalty barely bites, so it's about position and iron play, not power. Take dead aim, make the putts, and convert the 5s.
